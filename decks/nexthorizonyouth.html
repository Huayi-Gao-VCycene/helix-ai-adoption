The Problem
When 18 means
abandoned.
For most people, 18 is a celebration. For tens of thousands of young Canadians aging out of foster care — 18 is the day the support stops. Not slowly. All at once.
Housing support, mental health services, employment programs, mentorship — gone on one birthday
53% of youth who age out of care experience homelessness within 2 years
80,000+ young people in Ontario alone are not in education, employment, or training
We have a system that abandons people at the exact moment they need it most
Root Cause
The gap is
structural.
Programs end at 18 — but the brain isn't fully developed until 25. We withdraw support precisely when it's still needed.
Services are siloed — mental health here, housing there, employment somewhere else. A person in crisis can't navigate that alone.
Programs are time-limited — 3 months, 6 months. Youth build trust with a counsellor, then the program ends. Starting over is demoralizing.
Many never seek help at all — because asking feels like admitting failure
The Solution
Four pillars.
One continuous relationship.
Next Horizon Youth provides wrap-around support for youth ages 16 to 29 — the whole span of young adulthood, not until 18.
01 · Mentorship
Matched by lived experience — not authority figures, people who have been exactly where you are
02 · Life Skills
Practical and actionable, not theoretical — money, housing, relationships, health
03 · Employment
Resume, interview coaching, and job placement partnerships with local employers
04 · Community
Weekly group sessions. A place to belong — connection that combats isolation
Program Journey
No hard cutoff.
The relationship stays.
Week 1
Peer navigator assigned — someone who has been where they are. Together they build a support plan.
Mo. 1–3
Weekly 1-on-1 sessions, group life skills workshops, employment exploration
Mo. 3–12
Job placement support, housing stability check-ins, active community participation
Year 1+
Alumni become peer mentors. The cycle continues. No hard cutoff — as long as needed.
